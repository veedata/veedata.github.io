## Since you're here

You got a **404**, which is... boring. Let me tell you about **418: I'm a teapot**.

It comes from [RFC 2324](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2324), the Hyper Text
Coffee Pot Control Protocol, published on April 1st, 1998 — a joke specification
for controlling coffee pots over HTTP, complete with a `BREW` method and a
`coffee://` URI scheme. Buried in it is this:

> Any attempt to brew coffee with a teapot should result in the error code "418
> I'm a teapot". The resulting entity body MAY be short and stout.

[RFC 7168](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7168) extended the protocol to tea
in 2014, because, why not :)

In 2017 there was a serious proposal to strip 418 out of server frameworks for 
not being a real status code, and the internet revolted — 
[save418.com](https://save418.com/) turned it into a small campaign. It worked. 
The current HTTP spec,
[RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110#section-15.5.19), permanently
reserves 418 and explicitly says it must never be assigned to anything else.

Anyway. Still a 404. But now you know.
